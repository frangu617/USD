Frequency Lab

Explore your data with a frequency distribution, histogram, and descriptive statistics.

Paste a list of numbers or labels followed by values, such as Nation CO2 Albania 2.0 Australia 15.4. Spaces, tabs, commas, semicolons, and new lines are supported; names can contain spaces. Headers and labels are excluded. You can also paste a table such as income education race 16 10 B 18 7 B: click Analyze data, then choose a numeric column and optional group filter. Use single-word headers and NA for missing cells. Flattened tables must start each row with two numeric columns. For other layouts, import a CSV and select a column. Use a decimal point; omit thousands separators. Automatic bins use Sturges’ rule, capped at 100. Bin widths round up to clean increments (such as 0.5, 1, 2, 2.5, or 5); the axis may extend beyond the data.

Frequency curve

This frequency polygon connects the counts at each bin midpoint. It shows the observed distribution, rather than assuming a bell-shaped normal distribution. Changing the bins changes this curve.

Five-number summary

Q1 and Q3 are the medians of the lower and upper halves of the sorted data, excluding the overall median when the count is odd. For a single value, all five numbers equal that value.

Box plot

The box spans Q1 to Q3; its center line marks the median. Whiskers reach the most extreme observations within 1.5 × IQR of the box. Circles mark outliers, so whiskers may differ from the minimum and maximum.

Frequency distribution
